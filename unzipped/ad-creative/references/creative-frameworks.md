# Creative Frameworks Reference

Read this file when planning creative angles and writing ad copy. Select frameworks based on funnel stage and platform.

---

## Funnel Stage → Framework Selection

### Awareness — Lead with the Problem
They don't know you. Meet them where they are.

**Frame:** Problem → Amplify → Hint at Solution

- Lead with the pain, not the product
- Use the language they use when complaining to a colleague
- Don't pitch. Relate.

**Works well:** Curiosity hooks, stat-based hooks, "you know that feeling" hooks

**Visual approach:** Bold, attention-grabbing imagery. Problem visualization. Pattern interrupts. Dark/moody tones for pain points, bright reveals for the hint of solution.

### Consideration — Lead with the Solution
They know the problem. They're evaluating options.

**Frame:** Solution → Mechanism → Proof

- Explain what you do through the lens of the outcome they want
- Show that you work differently (the mechanism matters)
- Social proof starts mattering: reviews, case studies, numbers

**Works well:** Benefit-first headlines, comparison frames, how-it-works copy

**Visual approach:** Product in action. UI screenshots. Before/after layouts. Clean, confident design. Show the mechanism.

### Decision — Lead with Proof
They're close. Remove the last objection.

**Frame:** Proof → Risk Removal → Urgency

- Testimonials, case studies, results with numbers
- Remove risk: free trial, money-back, no credit card
- Only real urgency — not fake countdown timers

**Works well:** Social proof headlines, guarantee-first, before/after

**Visual approach:** Testimonial cards. Results/metrics visuals. Trust badges. Clean CTAs. Warm, confident colors.

## Headline Formulas

### Benefit-First
`[Verb] [specific outcome] [timeframe or qualifier]`

- "Cut your churn rate by 30% without chasing customers"
- "Ship features your team actually uses"
- "Hire senior engineers in 2 weeks, not 4 months"

### Curiosity
`[Surprising claim or counterintuitive angle]`

- "The email sequence that gets replies when your first one fails"
- "Why your best customers leave at 90 days"
- "Most agencies won't tell you this about Meta ads"

### Social Proof
`[Number] [people/companies] [outcome]`

- "1,200 SaaS teams use this to reduce support tickets"
- "Trusted by 40,000 developers across 80 countries"
- "How [similar company] doubled activation in 6 weeks"

### Urgency (done right)
`[Real scarcity or time-sensitive value]`

- "Q1 pricing ends March 31 — new rates from April 1"
- "Only 3 onboarding slots open this month"

Never: "🔥 LIMITED TIME DEAL!! ACT NOW!!!" — gets rejected, looks desperate.

### Problem Agitation
`[Describe the pain vividly]`

- "Still losing 40% of signups before they see value?"
- "Your ads are running, your budget is spending, and you're not sure what's working"

### Comparison / Alternative
`[Old way] vs. [New way with your product]`

- "Spreadsheets vs. actually knowing your numbers"
- "Stop hiring agencies. Start running your own ads in 20 minutes."

## Creative Angles Taxonomy

Use these angles to ensure variety across ad variants. Never produce all ads with the same angle.

| Angle | Description | Best For |
|-------|-------------|----------|
| **Problem-Agitation** | Vividly describe the pain the audience feels | Awareness |
| **Benefit-First** | Lead with the outcome, not the product | Consideration |
| **Social Proof** | Numbers, testimonials, logos, case studies | Decision |
| **Curiosity** | Counterintuitive or surprising claim | Awareness, Consideration |
| **How-It-Works** | Show the mechanism / product in action | Consideration |
| **Us vs. Them** | Compare against the old way or competitors | Consideration |
| **UGC / Testimonial** | Real person sharing their experience | All stages |
| **Urgency / Scarcity** | Time-limited or capacity-limited offer | Decision |
| **Authority** | Expert endorsement, data, research | Consideration, Decision |
| **Before/After** | Show transformation with proof | Consideration, Decision |

## Video Ad Structure: Hook → Body → CTA

### Hook (0–3 seconds)
**Job:** Stop the scroll. Create a micro-moment of "wait, what?"

**Visual hook types:**
- Pattern interrupt (unexpected visual, bold movement)
- Text overlay with bold claim
- Person speaking directly to camera (UGC style)
- Satisfying motion/transformation
- Product unboxing or reveal

**Text hook types:**
- Question that calls out the audience ("Tired of X?")
- Stat or number ("87% of teams fail at this")
- Bold claim ("We deleted our pricing page")
- Relatable complaint ("I used to spend 4 hours on reports")

### Body (3–10 seconds)
**Job:** Deliver one clear value point. NOT three. One.

- Show the product solving the problem from the hook
- Demonstrate the mechanism (how it works)
- Present social proof (results, testimonials)
- Keep it moving — new visual every 2-3 seconds

### CTA (last 2–3 seconds)
**Job:** Tell them exactly what to do.

- Clear action: "Try free for 14 days" / "See how it works" / "Book a demo"
- Visual: logo + URL + CTA button overlay
- Audio: reinforce the CTA verbally if using voiceover

## Carousel Ad Structure

### Story Arc (3–6 cards)
1. **Card 1 — Hook:** Stop the scroll. Bold question, surprising stat, or relatable problem
2. **Card 2–4 — Value:** Each card delivers one point (feature, benefit, proof point)
3. **Card 5 — Proof:** Testimonial, result, or social proof
4. **Card 6 — CTA:** Clear next step with branding

### Design Rules
- Consistent visual style across all cards (same colors, fonts, layout structure)
- Each card must work on its own AND as part of the sequence
- Use visual connectors (arrows, continuing backgrounds, numbered steps) to encourage swiping
- Text must be readable at mobile size — minimum 24pt equivalent

## Platform-Specific Creative Approaches

### Meta (Facebook / Instagram)
- Feed: Clean, thumb-stopping visuals. Minimal text on image. Let primary text do the talking.
- Stories/Reels: Full-screen vertical. Fast-paced. Native-feeling. UGC style wins.
- Carousel: Story-telling format. Each card swipeable and self-contained.

### Google Display
- Banner ads need to work at small sizes. Bold headline + simple visual + CTA button.
- Avoid clutter — these appear alongside content, not in a feed.
- High contrast between background and text.

### LinkedIn
- Professional tone but not boring. Thought leadership angle works.
- Single image with insight/stat + detailed intro text.
- Carousel for step-by-step frameworks or data stories.

### TikTok
- Must feel native. UGC/creator style. Vertical only for best results.
- Hook in first 1 second (not 3 — TikTok users are faster).
- Trending visual styles: green screen, text overlay storytelling, POV format.
- Raw > polished on this platform.

### YouTube
- Pre-roll: You have 5 seconds before skip. Make them count.
- Shorts: Same energy as TikTok but slightly more polished.
- Long-form: Can tell a complete story. Problem → Solution → Proof → CTA.

### Twitter / X
- Visual needs to work in a fast-scrolling text-heavy feed.
- Bold, simple imagery with clear headline.
- Conversational, direct tone.

## UGC-Style Creative Guidelines

UGC-style ads look authentic but are strategically crafted:

- **Camera:** Slightly imperfect framing, natural lighting, handheld feel
- **Talent:** Real person speaking to camera, casual setting (desk, kitchen, commute)
- **Script:** Conversational, first-person. "I was struggling with X, then I found Y"
- **Structure:** Still follows Hook → Body → CTA, just wrapped in casual delivery
- **Text overlays:** Bold captions (TikTok style) reinforcing key points
- **Audio:** Natural voice, not voiceover-polished

When generating UGC-style visuals with `image_generate`, prompt for:
- Natural, candid photography style
- Warm, authentic lighting (not studio-perfect)
- Real-world settings (office, home, coffee shop)
- Person interacting with product naturally
